// 01 — About

The engineer behind the shock layer.

I'm an M.Tech Aerospace Engineering student at IIT Bombay, focused on the regime where flight gets violent: hypersonic speeds, where the air ahead of a vehicle is crushed into a shock layer hot enough to glow, dissociate and chemically react.

My pull toward aerospace started with a simple question — how does anything survive re-entry? Chasing that answer led me into aerothermodynamics and heat transfer, into ANSYS Fluent and shock-tunnel data, and into writing my own scientific-computing tools when the off-the-shelf ones fell short.

Today my research interests span hypersonic aerodynamics, high-speed aerothermodynamics, CFD, shock waves, gas dynamics and machine learning for aerospace — and I build engineering software and AI research assistants to make that work faster and more reliable.

Long-term vision

To contribute to the design of next-generation hypersonic and re-entry vehicles — at organisations like ISRO, DRDO, NASA, SpaceX or Blue Origin — and to advance the science of aerothermal prediction through research that is rigorous, reproducible and useful.

// 04 — Master's Thesis

Predicting the heat of hypersonic flight.

Thesis · in progress
An Analytical Model to Estimate Heat-Transfer Rates on Hypersonic Vehicles

At hypersonic speeds the bow shock superheats the air into a chemically-reacting gas. Predicting how much of that heat reaches the surface — fast, without a full CFD campaign — is the difference between a viable thermal-protection design and a failed one. My thesis develops a fast analytical model, benchmarked against CFD across ideal-gas and real-gas (dissociating) regimes.

Ideal & real gasO₂ / N₂ dissociation 5-species air chemistryANSYS Fluent benchmarkFay–Riddell
01 · Motivation

Why it matters

Thermal-protection mass drives hypersonic vehicle feasibility. A fast, trustworthy heat-flux estimate shortens the design loop versus running full CFD for every configuration.

02 · Problem

Problem statement

Predict stagnation and distributed surface heat-transfer rates analytically, capturing real-gas chemistry effects that ideal-gas relations miss at high enthalpy.

03 · Literature

State of the art

Building on Fay–Riddell, Sutton–Graves and boundary-layer theory; reviewing where classical correlations diverge from reacting-flow CFD.

04 · Method

Methodology

Analytical formulation paired with a structured CFD workflow in ANSYS Fluent for benchmarking — geometry, mesh, solver, post-processing.

05 · Chemistry

Gas modelling

Ideal-gas → real-gas progression with oxygen and nitrogen dissociation and a five-species air model to capture high-temperature energy absorption.

06 · Contribution

Expected outcome

A validated, low-cost predictive model and a clear map of when real-gas effects must be included — with future work toward ML-accelerated surrogates.
